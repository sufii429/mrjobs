A Fresh Graduate's First Pharma Job: Realistic Expectations

MrJobs Blog · Published November 2, 2026

Most fresh graduates join pharma sales with two wrong pictures in mind: either it is easy money for visiting doctors, or it is a stopgap until a "real" job appears. Both pictures collapse in the first month. This is the honest version — what your first year as a Medical Representative in Pakistan actually looks like, so you can decide with open eyes and survive the start that defeats so many beginners.

📍 Your first month is school, not selling

Expect one to four weeks of induction training before you work alone: your company's products, the science behind them, and hours of detailing practice in front of trainers who interrupt you exactly like real doctors. Then come joint field days, where your manager or a senior rep watches your calls and corrects you in the corridor afterwards. Nobody expects sales miracles in month one. What they are watching is whether you show up on time, learn the products, and write honest reports. (Our guide to becoming an MR walks through the full entry route.)

📌 Money: what actually reaches your pocket

A fresh MR's total package in Pakistan typically lands around PKR 50,000–80,000 a month once basic salary, TA/DA and mobile allowance are combined — multinationals at the top of the range, smaller local companies below it. Two warnings. First, your field expenses (fuel, food, mobile data) are spent before allowances and reimbursements arrive, so keep a small buffer for the first two months. Second, treat incentive as a bonus, not a budget: in your first quarters you will miss some slabs while you learn the territory, and the reps who budget on incentive money are the ones borrowing by month three. The full breakdown is in our salary and incentives guide.

📝 The routine nobody warns you about

  • Split days. Hospital OPDs run in the morning; private clinics run in the evening. Your working day has a long middle gap and two working ends — plan rest and reporting inside the gap instead of fighting it.
  • Waiting is the job. A two-minute detailing call can take forty minutes of sitting outside a clinic. Carry your product notes; the waiting room is where tomorrow's calls get prepared.
  • Rejection is routine. Doctors will refuse, postpone, and sometimes be blunt. It is about their schedule, not your worth. The reps who last are the ones whose routine does not bend with their mood.
  • Reporting is non-negotiable. Daily call reports, chemist checks, and expense claims are how a manager sees you. Sloppy reporting gets noticed months before weak sales do.

📌 Targets and pressure — the honest version

Yes, there is pressure: monthly targets are the centre of this job, and some months you will miss. What matters is the pattern. A new territory honestly takes a quarter to start moving; managers know this and judge your coverage — are you seeing the right doctors regularly, is stock available at the right chemists — before they judge your numbers. Pressure becomes dangerous only when reps start faking calls or reports to escape it. Never do this. A missed target is a bad month; a caught fake report is a finished career in that city's small pharma circle.

📝 Five mistakes that end first years early

  • Visiting only friendly doctors and skipping the difficult, high-potential ones.
  • Skipping chemist visits, so prescribed brands are out of stock when patients arrive.
  • Comparing your package with a cousin in a multinational every single week.
  • Spending the first incentive before the second quarter proves it repeats.
  • Quitting at month four — exactly when the territory was about to start knowing your face.

📍 What a good first year looks like

By month twelve, a solid fresh rep knows 150-plus doctors by name and habit, holds a reporting record nobody questions, and finishes the year somewhere near target — with the last two quarters visibly stronger than the first two. That trajectory, more than any single month's number, is what earns the Senior MR discussion and the better territory. Pharma sales rewards the patient: the field is full of Area Managers who began exactly where you are, doubting exactly what you are doubting. Give the job one honest year before you judge it — and judge it by your last quarter, not your first.

📍 Insider Tip: Keep a small notebook for every doctor: timings, preferred products, one personal detail (a child's school, a cricket team). Remembering one detail on the next visit opens more doors than any detailing aid.
