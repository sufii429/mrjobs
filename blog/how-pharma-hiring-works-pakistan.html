How Pharma Hiring Works in Pakistan — The MR to AM Ladder

MrJobs Blog · Published November 2, 2026

Every Medical Representative opening on this board exists because somewhere a territory fell vacant or a company decided to grow. Understanding how pharma companies hire — who interviews you, why the vacancy opened, and what the next rung looks like — tells you how to apply, whom you will actually work for, and how fast your career can move. Here is the system from the inside.

🧬 The ladder, rung by rung

  • Medical Representative (MR / MIO / SPO). The ground level: one rep, one territory, a list of doctors and chemists, and a monthly target. Almost everyone in pharma management started here.
  • Senior MR. The same field job with a stronger record — bigger or more important doctors, sometimes mentoring a new rep. The jump is earned on achievement percentages, not just years.
  • Territory Manager (TM / STM). Still field-based in most Pakistani companies, but with a premium territory, specialty portfolio, or a small team in larger setups. This is the first rung where package negotiations get serious.
  • Area Sales Manager (AM / ASM). The first true management rung: a team of four to eight reps, joint field work, and responsibility for the whole area's numbers. Company cars usually start here.
  • Regional / Zonal Sales Manager (RSM / ZSM). Several areas, several managers. Hiring at this level is about judgement — of people and territories — as much as sales history.
  • National Sales Manager (NSM) and above. Strategy, launches, and the national number. Reached by a minority — but almost always by people who climbed every rung below it.

📍 Why territories fall vacant

A vacancy is a story. The previous rep resigned (the most common reason — reps move for better packages and territories), was promoted, or the company created a new territory by splitting a large one or launching a new product line. Expansion vacancies are the best seats: a new territory has no legacy problems, and a launch team gets management attention. A territory that falls vacant again and again in a year is a warning sign — ask the outgoing rep's story politely during your interview if you can.

📝 Who actually interviews you

In most Pakistani pharma companies, hiring is manager-led. Your first and most important interview is with the Area or Regional Sales Manager you would report to — he is judging whether he can work a territory with you. HR usually enters later, for documentation, degree verification, and the offer letter. In multinationals and top nationals, expect a longer chain: a screening interview, sometimes a short written test of science and English, a second interview with the Zonal or National Sales Manager, and only then HR. The manager interview decides; the rest confirms.

📌 What each promotion really requires

  • MR → Senior MR / TM: two to four years of consistent target achievement in the same territory, a clean reporting record, and a doctor network that visibly produces prescriptions.
  • TM → AM: proof that you can make others perform — joint-work coaching, honest appraisals of colleagues, and numbers held together when a team member left.
  • AM → RSM: area-level thinking — territory design, distributor handling, and hiring judgement. Managers who hired well get promoted; managers who hired carelessly carry the vacancies with them.

📝 How to use this when you apply

Apply to the rung you can prove, not the rung you want. A fresh graduate applying to TM seats wastes everyone's time; an MR with three years above 100% who keeps applying to MR seats undersells himself. When a listing on MrJobs names a designation, match your CV's first lines to it — designation, segment, territory — because the first-line manager who reads it is scanning for exactly those three things. Our interview guide covers what he will ask, and the salary guide covers what each rung pays.

📍 Insider Tip: The fastest climbers treat every territory like a business they own — they know their top doctors, their chemists' stock, and their numbers without opening a file. Managers promote the rep they would trust with their own old territory.
